01

Resume & Impact Bullets

A recruiter spends ~10 seconds — earn them
1.1 — Structure (one page, scannable)

Order: Contact → Summary (2 lines) → Skills → Experience (most space) → Projects → Education. Reverse-chronological, consistent formatting, no photo, PDF.

1.2 — The impact-bullet formula
📖 XYZ / STAR for bullets
"Accomplished [X], measured by [Y], by doing [Z]." Lead with an action verb, attach a number, name the tech. Quantify even with estimates.
❌
Weak (duty)
"Responsible for working on APIs and fixing bugs."
✅
Strong (impact)
"Cut API p95 latency 40% by adding Redis caching and fixing N+1 queries in EF Core, serving 50K+ daily requests."
→ verb + number + tech + outcome.
💡 You already have the vocabulary
Every bullet can now cite real concepts from this course — caching (M17), N+1 (M10), async scalability (M9), CI/CD (M22). Reframe existing work using this language; you don't need a new job to write stronger bullets.
02

ATS & Your Narrative

Get past the filter, tell a clear story
📖 ATS (Applicant Tracking System)
Software that screens resumes before a human sees them, often matching keywords from the job description. Beat it with a clean single-column layout (no tables/graphics that break parsing), standard section headings, and the exact terms from the posting where they're true: ".NET 8", "ASP.NET Core", "EF Core", "Redis", "Docker", "microservices", "REST APIs".
💡 Tailor per application
Mirror each job description's language. If it says "RESTful services" use that phrasing; if "CI/CD pipelines", ensure those words appear. Small edits, big difference in pass-through rate.
Your legacy → modern narrative

Frame the transition as a strength, not a gap: "4 years building and maintaining production .NET systems, recently deepening modern .NET 8 — Clean Architecture, EF Core, containerised microservices, and cloud deployment." Maintaining legacy systems shows you understand real-world constraints; the modern study shows initiative. That combination is attractive.

03

LinkedIn

Where recruiters find you
  • ✓Headline — not just "Software Engineer": "Backend Engineer · .NET 8 · ASP.NET Core · Azure · Microservices". Keywords recruiters search.
  • ✓About — 3–4 lines: who you are, your stack, what you're looking for.
  • ✓Experience — mirror your resume's impact bullets.
  • ✓Skills — list the keywords; they're searchable and endorsable.
  • ✓"Open to work" — set it (recruiter-only visibility is an option); a clear photo and complete profile rank higher.
📝 Optional edge
A pinned GitHub with a clean .NET 8 project (Clean Architecture + Docker + tests + a README) demonstrates everything in this course better than any bullet. Even one polished repo stands out.

4.2 — Behavioural with STAR
📖 STAR
Situation → Task → Action → Result. Prepare 5–6 stories (a hard bug, a conflict, a tight deadline, a leadership/ownership moment, a failure you learned from) and map them to common prompts. End with a measurable result.
💡 Mock practice
Practise out loud — explain a concept, solve a DSA problem narrating your thinking (M25), and walk an HLD (M27) on paper. Record yourself or pair with a peer. Speaking fluently is a separate skill from knowing the answer, and it's trainable.
05

Application Strategy

Maximising and converting your shots
  • ✓Referrals first — a referral hugely outperforms a cold application. Reconnect with ex-colleagues; politely ask. It's the single highest-leverage move.
  • ✓Volume + consistency — apply steadily; it's a numbers game with a low per-application hit rate. Track applications in a sheet.
  • ✓Target fit — apply even at ~70% match; job descriptions are wish-lists. Product companies on modern .NET fit your goal range.
  • ✓Interview in waves — cluster interviews so you have offers in parallel for leverage, and so you warm up on lower-priority targets first.
  • ✓Negotiate — you can almost always negotiate; competing offers and market data (Glassdoor/levels) are your leverage. Be polite, specific, and patient.
⚠️ Don't stop prepping after one offer
Keep your pipeline alive until you've signed. More options means better leverage and a calmer mindset — which itself improves interview performance.

Putting It Together

The 12-week journey, recapped
①
Phase 1 (M1–16)
Modern .NET mastery — HTTP, Core architecture, DI, EF Core, SQL, auth, Clean Architecture, SOLID, patterns, testing.
②
Phase 2 (M17–24)
Scalable backend & cloud — caching/Redis, performance, microservices, messaging, Docker, CI/CD, Azure, observability.
③
Phase 3 (M25–30)
Interview mode — DSA, system design (fundamentals/HLD/LLD), Git, and this career-conversion module.
💡 How it all connects
Notice the recurring threads: statelessness (REST → caching → scaling → cloud), idempotency (HTTP → messaging → microservices), dependency inversion (DI → SOLID → Clean Architecture → LLD). Interviewers love when you connect concepts across domains — that's what separates a 4 YOE engineer from a junior who learned topics in isolation.
